Short learning trajectories are often interpreted as evidence of stable optimization and policy convergence, but this inference can fail in partially observable multi-agent reinforcement learning. When relational variables such as teammate identity, role, or task assignment are hidden, gradients that are useful within individual relational contexts are projected onto a coarser information space and may cancel, causing the policy to move little not because it has converged, but because it lacks the information required to improve. We formalize this failure mode as Information-Induced Gradient Contraction, derive a Pythagorean decomposition of relation-conditioned gradient energy, and introduce the Relational Gradient Retention Ratio, \kappa. Together with a drift–diffusion analysis, \kappa yields a Stable-or-Stuck diagnostic that distinguishes convergence from information-induced stagnation. An analytic latent-relation coordination problem exhibits the extreme case of zero mixed gradient, a nearly stationary learning path, and chance-level return. Across 22 PPO runs in 510K, hiding relations produces shorter behavioral trajectories than revealing them (0.293 \pm 0.049 vs. 0.328 \pm 0.062); continuous information-reveal interventions and direct gradient measurements further support the mechanism. Partial observability can therefore erase useful learning signals, not merely increase gradient noise.
