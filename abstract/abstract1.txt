Short learning trajectories are often taken as evidence of stable optimization and policy convergence. We show that, in partially observable multi-agent reinforcement learning, they may instead signal a failure to learn. When relational variables such as teammate identity, role, or task assignment are hidden, gradients that are useful within different relational contexts are projected onto the same local information space and can cancel. We formalize this phenomenon as Information-Induced Gradient Contraction, derive a Pythagorean decomposition of relation-conditioned gradient energy, and introduce the Relational Gradient Retention Ratio, \kappa, to quantify how much useful learning signal survives partial observability. Combined with a drift–diffusion analysis, \kappa yields a Stable-or-Stuck diagnostic that distinguishes genuine convergence from information-induced stagnation. Analytic constructions, controlled information-reveal interventions, and direct gradient measurements consistently support the proposed mechanism. Our results reveal that partial observability can erase useful gradients, not merely make them noisier: a short learning trajectory may therefore indicate that the gradients required for improvement have been averaged away.
